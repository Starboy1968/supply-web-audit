# Portfolio: Supply Web Audit Competence

This framework demonstrates competence across **Risk, Supply, and GRC** — three domains that matter for owner-operated SMBs but are rarely audited together.

---

## Why This Exists

Most audits are siloed:
- Risk audits look at threats and controls (cyber, financial, operational)
- Supply audits track vendors and concentrations (sourcing, continuity)
- GRC audits check compliance and governance (regulations, policies, board reporting)

They miss the connection: **a vendor failure is a risk that hits your supply, triggers GRC exposure, and compounds under regulatory stress.**

This framework audits the web as one system. It answers the question that matters: **Can we survive if X fails, and what does that cost us?**

---

## What This Demonstrates

### Risk Competence
- **Game theory thinking** — Players, payoffs, infinite games. Treats suppliers/partners as actors with their own incentives, not static assets.
- **Shock testing** — Models failures before they happen. "If AWS region fails, how long until customer impact?"
- **Narrow point identification** — Finds chokepoints and single points of failure, ranked by speed-of-consequence.

### Supply Competence
- **Vendor mapping** — 40+ integrations, tiers, concentration risk. Knows who's replaceable, who's critical, who has no backup.
- **Credit tracing** — Follows money: who has cash, who's leveraged, where does credit dry up? Recovery cost modeling.
- **Concentration analysis** — Top customer exposure vs. SLA liability. Can we absorb a major loss?

### GRC Competence
- **Regulatory exposure** — GDPR, SOX, state privacy, breach notification timelines. What keeps the board awake?
- **Compliance gaps** — Insurance coverage, contract fine print, incident response playbooks. What's missing?
- **Re-audit triggers** — "When do we audit again?" Set by: new regulation, new vendor, incident, policy change, elapsed time.

## How This Works

**8-stage pipeline.** Each stage has a human gate — nothing moves forward until you've read and approved the output.

1. **Scope** — Fix the boundary (what's in, what's out, what we don't know)
2. **Players & AI** — Roster all players, map who uses what AI
3. **Link Ledger** — One row per relationship (who buys from whom, terms, risk flags)
4. **Money Map** — Trace credit and buffers (who has cash, insurance, recovery cost)
5. **Narrow Points** — Find where web is thin (chokepoints, regulatory blockers, key person gaps)
6. **Behavior** — Read records (incident history, patch delays, access discipline, slack)
7. **Test Moves** — Model scenarios (if X fails, how long until impact, who survives)
8. **Report** — Signed output + re-audit triggers

**Timeline:** 2–4 weeks (depends on data availability).

**Evidence standard:** Every claim labeled Established / Author's construction / Gap. Board knows what to trust.

## The Game Theory Foundation

This framework rests on one insight: **supply webs are infinite games.**

Linear thinking (A→B→C→customer) assumes stability. Real supply webs are meshed. Players recycle. New entrants arrive. Old relationships break.

Game theory approach:
- Don't ask "what are our risks?" Ask "what changes other players' payoffs?"
- Don't ask "who are our vendors?" Ask "who, if removed, changes the options available to others?"
- Don't assume stability. Build resilience into the web itself.

This thinking applies to cyber risk, supply chain, operational continuity, financial stress — anywhere a system has multiple interdependent players.
